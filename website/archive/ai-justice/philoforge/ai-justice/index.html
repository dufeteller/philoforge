When production depends less on work

If AI can sustain production and innovation with less human labor, income from work may cease to give everyone access to what society produces. The decisive question becomes who owns the infrastructure, who receives its output, and who sets its priorities.

A sufficiently productive common infrastructure could support universal access and democratic economic choices. Its feasibility is an explicit hypothesis, not a consequence of the current capabilities of AI.

Essential provision, with an optional stronger criterion

The foundational commitments are equal liberties and effective democracy (J1), and universal essential provision whenever it is feasible (J3). The earlier maximin criterion (J2) remains available as an optional strengthening, with all its existing results.

Lean proves that maximin implies the sufficiency commitment and that the converse fails. These moral commitments are declared choices. Formal verification checks their consequences, not their moral authority.

What the formalization establishes

Lean checks conditional deductions over exact rational quantities. It does not prove that automation eliminates work, that a majority will lose, or that communism is uniquely superior. Collective ownership, mixed systems and broad private ownership must be compared through their actual feasibility, needs coverage and democratic guarantees.
